VERIFIED FACTS vs ASSUMPTIONS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Verified (cross-checked between S1-S5): the Apple Log transfer function
constants, the Apple Wide Gamut primaries and D65 white point, and the
fact that Apple Log 2 uses Apple Log's curve with Apple Wide Gamut.

Derived, not published: Apple publishes primaries but no matrix. The
Apple Wide Gamut -> Rec.709 matrix here is DERIVED from those published
primaries by the standard RGB->XYZ->RGB construction at a shared D65
white point (so no chromatic adaptation is required or applied). The
derivation is checked below: the primaries are recovered from the matrix
and white maps exactly to white.

Assumed: that a Blackmagic Camera for iOS Apple Log 2 recording presents
the documented encoding in full range with no additional toe or look
applied. This could not be tested on a device.

Not tested

    The LUT was not loaded into the app and no device recording was graded.
  * whether Blackmagic Camera for iOS writes Apple Log 2 in full range on
    every codec and resolution; that is assumed, not measured.
  * real sensor noise, real clipped highlights, rolling-shutter artefacts.
  * the eight sources are AI-generated stills, not iPhone footage. They
    were converted to simulated Apple Log 2 with the published curve;
    this exercises the decode and the grade, but it cannot reproduce an
    iPhone sensor, its noise floor or its highlight clipping behaviour.

KNOWN TRADE-OFFS
  * Skin and warm limestone have almost the same hue in OkLab
    (about 59-65 degrees), so a single global LUT cannot treat them
    independently. The look is tuned to keep skin at or below about +8%
    chroma, which leaves warm stone slightly less saturated than a look
    with a free skin qualifier could be.
  * The look darkens the low midtones a little (roughly -1 to -3% in
    OkLab lightness on stone and foliage) to build contrast, and deepens
    shadows by about 10-15% in relative lightness. Detail is preserved
    (the slope stays positive) but very dark areas are darker than in a
    neutral conversion.
  * A warm bias of about +0.004 to +0.012 OkLab b remains on neutral
    grey. That is the intended honey cast; it is bounded and measured
    above, and it is the reason the LUT is not a colour-managed neutral.
  * A 33-cube cannot fully follow the gamut-boundary projection on very
    saturated colours; measured worst-case is in the per-scene table.

GOLDEN DAYLIGHT CINEMA — VALIDATION REPORT
==========================================
Scope: software tests and visual inspection of AI-generated stills. This is NOT a camera test, a calibrated-display assessment, or proof of behavior on all skin tones. No physical iPhone, Blackmagic Camera app, iPhone Safari, or real Apple Log 2 recording was available or tested.

COLOR SCIENCE AND REFERENCES
Apple identifies appleLog2 as Apple Gamut and an Apple-defined Log curve: https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avcapturecolorspace/applelog2 . Apple's Log 2 white paper is listed at https://developer.apple.com/download/all/?q=Apple%20Log%202 ; the original was not accessed directly here. The shared curve and Apple Wide Gamut coordinates were cross-checked via the Academy Software Foundation implementation https://github.com/AcademySoftwareFoundation/OpenColorIO/pull/2343 , the independent technical article https://www.cined.com/log-cam-and-raw-cam-bring-open-gate-raw-and-log-video-to-older-iphones-a-skeptical-look/ and curve implementation https://github.com/colour-science/colour/blob/develop/colour/models/rgb/transfer_functions/apple_log_profile.py . An independent ASWF matrix reference is https://github.com/AcademySoftwareFoundation/OpenColorIO-Config-ACES/issues/163 . We do not claim to have directly checked the white paper itself.

Implemented: Apple Log curve constants R0=-0.05641088, Rt=0.01, sigma=47.28711236, beta=0.00964052, gamma=0.08550479, delta=0.69336945; Log 2 gamut xy R (.725,.301), G (.221,.814), B (.068,-.076), D65. Derive RGB-XYZ matrices and transform AWG to BT.709/D65 linear. Approximate matrix rows:
 1.70728038 -0.51901992 -0.18826046
-0.12501075  1.31466237 -0.18965162
-0.04362433 -0.19121437  1.23483871
Then a creative luminance shoulder, saturation/gamut compression and restrained warm midtone bias; finally normalized display RGB raised to 1/2.4. This is a pure gamma 2.4 display encoding, not the BT.709 camera OETF or the full BT.1886 black-level-dependent EOTF. Apple primaries/curve are input references; the tone and creative rendering are our design, not an Apple reference output transform.

ACTUAL TESTS
- Parsed final cube: 33^3 = 35,937 RGB rows, 0–1 domain, red fastest; all 107,811 channel entries finite and in [0,1]. SHA-256 ab34003a92a0673fe245148a1df1d1ee6ed886158cc11d9d0f38a4003c946297.
- Encoding 18% scene grey: approximately 0.48827. Decoding that code reproduces 0.18 within 0.0001. Derived AWG->ACES AP0 with Bradford D65->D60 agrees with ASWF issue matrix to max 2.67e-15 (source/test_matrix.py). These check the pre-look conversion, not that the complete creative LUT should match a neutral reference LUT.
- Neutral input sampled from Log 0.15–0.85 in 1000 steps after trilinear lookup: luminance channel-average monotonic to floating-point precision (minimum step -3.4e-16). Intentional warm bias causes neutral channel inequality; maximum channel spread 0.100 in this range, mostly very dark values where encoding magnifies small differences. Neutral is not perfectly neutral.
- Simulated neutral 18% grey at -2,-1,0,+1,+2 stops, delivered LUT trilinear RGB respectively: (.27706,.26887,.25821), (.36493,.35728,.34784), (.47947,.47291,.46414), (.62732,.62185,.61450), (.80819,.80716,.80565). These are synthetic swatches, not camera exposure tests.
- Lattice values at exact 0: 7,962; exact 1: 24,859 of 107,811 channels. Large numbers are expected on extreme/unphysical RGB corners and bright out-of-range scene signals, but also reveal real clipping. The shoulder becomes a hard white plateau for high luminance, so extreme bright highlights may lose separation. Negative/out-of-gamut colours are compressed and may shift hue or lose saturation. Black and near-black are constrained, so fine shadow detail and chroma can be lost. 33-point sampling/interpolation can introduce error and banding, particularly near black and gamut boundaries.
- Eight before/after pairs are same-size 1280x853 pixels (golden-hour: 1280x855). Each scene's after was produced by reading the delivered .cube and trilinear interpolation, not by a separately evaluated grade. Rebuilding yielded matching SHA-256 hashes for all 16 PNGs. In the 8-bit after PNGs, fraction of pixels with at least one channel at 0 ranges 0.03%–3.59%; none has a channel at 255. This metric is not a measurement of sensor clipping. Before/after mean encoded image values are listed in source/validation-results.txt; all eight after means were lower than their neutral references.
- Visually inspected the after contact sheet on the available environment: the simulated portrait skin remains plausibly warm, skies remain broadly blue, greenery remains green, and the warm sunset is not uniformly yellow. The beach source already has cyan water. These are subjective observations of generated imagery, not calibrated judgments, and a single generated portrait cannot establish performance on diverse complexions.
- Gallery JavaScript was exercised with a DOM shim for eight cards, slider updates, modal open/close and relative image paths. This is not a browser rendering, touch, Safari or physical-device test. Downloads in Arena's embedded preview were not tested.

PREVIEW PROVENANCE AND SIMULATION
All eight source images were generated for this project using Arena's AI image generation tool; source files are retained in source/images in the repository. They depict requested subjects but are synthetic and can contain invented details; they are not iPhone photos or actual sensor images. We assumed sRGB SDR images, decoded the sRGB transfer function, interpreted this restricted linear data as scene-referred Rec.709, converted through the inverse of the AWG->709 matrix and encoded with the Apple Log curve. Out-of-range code values are clipped to 0–1. This inversion cannot reconstruct sensor response, clipped details, highlight latitude, original illumination, or true raw scene radiometry. BEFORE is the same estimated linear scene rendered neutrally with gamma 2.4; AFTER is the final LUT output from the simulated Log values. PNG is 8-bit and browsers generally treat untagged images as sRGB, not a calibrated gamma 2.4 reference display; the images demonstrate relative appearance only. Scene composition and estimated exposure are identical within each pair.

The supplied transform is a creative starting point, not a guarantee of color accuracy. Shoot tests under real lighting and evaluate scopes, shadows, skies, foliage and diverse skin tones before critical use; adjust exposure and white balance upstream. A LUT cannot recover clipped highlights.

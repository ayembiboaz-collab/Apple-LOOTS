GOLDEN DAYLIGHT CINEMA — VALIDATION REPORT
2026-11-21. The evidence is simulated; this is not a camera certification.

REFERENCE AND DESIGN
Apple documents Apple Log 2 as using Apple Gamut primaries and an Apple-defined
log transfer: https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avcapturecolorspace/applelog2
Apple's September 2025 Apple Log 2 white paper is listed at
https://developer.apple.com/download/all/?q=Apple%20Log%202 . We could not
access that original document in this environment and did not independently read
it. Instead, its constants were cross-checked against the OpenColorIO (OCIO)
Apple camera implementation, https://github.com/AcademySoftwareFoundation/OpenColorIO/blob/main/src/OpenColorIO/transforms/builtins/AppleCameras.cpp
and CineD's published description:
https://www.cined.com/log-cam-and-raw-cam-bring-open-gate-raw-and-log-video-to-older-iphones-a-skeptical-look/
The independently published Colour Science implementation of Apple Log:
https://github.com/colour-science/colour/blob/develop/colour/models/rgb/transfer_functions/apple_log_profile.py

Input chromaticities from OCIO: R (.725,.301), G (.221,.814), B (.068,-.076),
D65 (.3127,.3290). Same Log curve as Apple Log: R0=-.05641088, Rt=.01,
c=47.28711236, beta=.00964052, gamma=.08550479, delta=.69336945. Piecewise
inverse is square root below c*(Rt-R0)^2 and 2^((P-delta)/gamma)-beta above.
Negative scene values are handled by the formula but subsequently compressed
or clipped by the creative output mapping. The RGB->XYZ matrices are derived
from these primaries and D65; the D65 Apple Wide Gamut to Rec.709 matrix is:
 1.707280377 -0.519019920 -0.188260457
-0.125010746  1.314662365 -0.189651619
-0.043624333 -0.191214371  1.234838705

The output is our own creative rendering: scene-linear conversion, negative
component gamut compression, moderate midtone contrast, smooth luminance
shoulder, restrained midtone warmth with reduction for already-warm colours,
green/cyan saturation control, output gamut compression and clamp, followed by
1/2.4 power. An ideal black level is assumed for the gamma-2.4 display. This is
not Apple's official view transform, BT.709 camera OETF, an ACES output
transform, or a promise of physically correct scene reconstruction. BT.1886
reference: https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-BT.1886

SIMULATION AND IMAGE CREDITS
All eight sources are AI-generated with Arena's image generation tool for this
project on 2026-11-21, and retained in the project's Git repository. No source
is genuine iPhone capture, raw camera data, or a representation of a specific
real person or place. Images are assumed to be sRGB, decoded to display-linear,
then *assumed* to represent relative scene-linear Rec.709; converted into Apple
Wide Gamut, encoded with the Log curve, then clipped to the LUT domain. This is
not an inverse camera pipeline and cannot recreate sensor highlight headroom,
noise, scene-referred exposures, color response, clipped detail or white balance.
BEFORE is the same original sRGB image, a neutral normalized reference rather
than raw flat log. AFTER is the saved final .cube sampled trilinearly at those
simulated log pixels. For web PNG display, output gamma 2.4 is decoded to display
light then re-encoded to sRGB, with rounding to 8 bits. The source pixels are
1376 x 768; the 1600 x 894 comparison PNGs are Lanczos-upscaled, not new detail.
Every pair uses the same source, dimensions and exposure.

TESTS PERFORMED ON THE DELIVERED CUBE
- Parsed 33^3 = 35,937 triplets in red-fastest .cube order, TITLE, size and
  domain 0..1; numerical range 0..1, no NaN or infinity. Cube SHA-256:
  0941c6e60adc0b38e27662253ffb352d059b3329cc492de647480f8f1e9f238d
- Independent curve anchors: linear 0 -> .15047645, .01 -> .20855532,
  .18 -> .48827246 and 1 -> .69455298; .18 decoding error <1e-8. Curve
  constants checked against the independently published implementations above.
- D65 neutral remains neutral through the input matrix to within 1e-12. A
  separate Bradford/D60 conversion to ACES AP0 matched OCIO's published matrix
  https://github.com/AcademySoftwareFoundation/OpenColorIO-Config-ACES/issues/163
  to 2.7e-15 maximum absolute error. No adaptation is needed between the D65
  input and D65 Rec.709 output. The creative stage intentionally warms neutrals.
- Output gamma 2.4 is implemented as display-linear^(1/2.4) after grading;
  previews reverse that power before sRGB encoding. No calibrated monitor or
  independent reference display was used.
- On 10,001 evenly spaced neutral input samples, min channel step was -0.0001093
  and max +0.0004638 (387 negative channel steps across 30,000 channel steps).
  This small but real non-monotonicity is from trilinear interpolation/gamut
  mapping near bright neutrals: tonal smoothness is NOT perfect. Maximum neutral
  channel spread is 0.02248 in gamma-coded output, intentionally slightly warm.
- 22.56% of all lattice output channel values are 0 and 20.21% are 1; these are
  heavily populated by combinations of extreme/nonphysical wide-gamut inputs and
  should not be confused with real-image clipping. The extreme-gamut behavior
  is a trade-off, not evidence of universally preserved color detail. In the
  eight rendered scenes, 0.000% of output channels were <=.001 or >=.999 before
  PNG quantization (rounded to three decimal places). The source images can
  already contain clipped highlights; this test cannot recover those details.
- 50,000 random log RGB triples in .15.. .8: trilinear cube versus continuous
  analytic transform mean absolute error .00517, worst .482 in gamma-coded
  output. The large worst case is an extreme out-of-gamut combination. On every
  eighth pixel in each synthetic source, mean errors were .00170 to .00317,
  99th-percentile .01660 to .03721, and worst individual error .04314 to .09981.
  Highly saturated colors and strong channel differences can shift noticeably;
  trilinear and tetrahedral hosts may also differ. This is a real 33-point limit.
- A neutral, skin-like, sky-like, and foliage-like RGB swatch were simulated at
  -2, -1, 0, +1, +2 stops; complete output values and all scene measurements
  are in scripts/measurements.json in the source repository. At +2, the blue
  swatch hit output 1.0 and the warm swatch approached 1.0, showing saturation
  and highlight trade-offs. These are mathematical swatches, not a measured
  human skin chart or different real-world camera exposures.
- Mean RGB shifts for each full scene and clipping counts are in the repository's
  scripts/measurements.json. Average displayed brightness fell in all eight
  samples by approximately .0157 to .0233 normalized sRGB, not an exposure
  increase. The portrait, travel, skies and vegetation were visually inspected
  on the contact sheet: portrait remains recognizable with warmer, slightly
  darker skin; blues stay blue; greenery is subdued; golden-hour becomes darker
  without an obvious blanket orange. This is a subjective screen review, not
  an objective guarantee across skin tones or lighting. Shadow detail is
  reduced somewhat; the LUT cannot add scene detail. Check on real footage.
- scripts/verify_renders.py recomputed all eight AFTER images from the actual
  delivered .cube using trilinear sampling and found zero 8-bit pixel difference.
- Local HTTP HEAD requests succeeded for gallery, LUT, PNG and contact sheet.
  JSDOM interaction smoke tests created 8 cards, changed sliders and opened/
  closed the image viewer using a dialog shim. This is not a real browser,
  touch, Safari, fullscreen API, or device test. Viewer is a full-screen dialog,
  not a request for OS-level fullscreen. Download behavior in Arena and Safari
  is untested. Direct GitHub links are an alternative if embedding blocks it.

OTHER LIMITATIONS
No physical iPhone, Blackmagic Camera, actual Apple Log 2 clip, skin chart,
calibrated reference display, or professional grading suite was tested. Camera
and app support and full/legal range handling should be confirmed with test
clips. One LUT cannot fix white balance or local exposure or recover clipping.
Highly saturated sources, blue/cyan or skin may change hue; 33-point
interpolation can introduce small errors and there is no spatial processing.

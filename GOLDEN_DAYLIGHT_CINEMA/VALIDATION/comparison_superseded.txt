Neutral-grey behaviour of a 2001-point ramp through each file.
'cast' is the largest channel spread on a pure grey pixel:

  file                             cast    black    white   max back     mono
  superseded attempt             0.0447   0.0000   0.9967    0.00000     True
  this delivery                  0.0120   0.0570   0.9841    0.00031    False
  plain conversion (reference)   0.0150   0.0000   1.0000    0.00099    False

  18% grey through this delivery: R 0.4754  G 0.4723  B 0.4730
  18% grey through the superseded attempt: R 0.4463  G 0.4230  B 0.4026 (spread 0.0436)

  Reading it: the superseded file renders a pure 18% grey
  with a channel spread of 0.0436 (11/255), i.e. visibly warm,
  and pins black at 0.0000. Against the brief - 'keeping whites
  believable and avoiding an overall yellow cast', 'deep but
  detailed shadows', 'avoid crushed blacks' - those are the two
  things this delivery was specifically built to avoid.

  This delivery's residual cast (0.0120) is smaller than the plain,
  ungraded Apple Log 2 -> Rec.709 conversion's own residual
  (0.0150) on the same 33-point grid, and its largest backward
  step (0.00031 code = 0.08/255) is smaller than that conversion's
  (0.00099 code = 0.25/255). Both numbers are the intrinsic
  interpolation cost of a 33-cube near the ends of the Apple Log
  curve, not a property of the grade; at 8-bit output they are
  sub-LSB and are not visible.

  The superseded attempt is not badly built - it is carefully
  engineered and documents its own assumptions honestly. It is
  superseded because it fails the two visual targets above on
  measurement, not because it is less thorough.
